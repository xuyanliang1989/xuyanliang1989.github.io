## What to look for

- **Thickness and cushioning.** Side sleepers need real loft under the hip and shoulder — generally 3 inches or more — so the pad can absorb pressure without bottoming out. A thin pad will always transmit the ground straight into your joint.
- **R-value and insulation.** Your hip compresses the pad, and compressed foam insulates poorly. If you camp in shoulder seasons or cold weather, look for an R-value of 3.5 or higher, and pay attention to whether the insulation runs the full length.
- **Baffle design and stability.** Vertical baffles, a hybrid baffle pattern, or a quilted surface keeps you from rolling toward the edge. Narrow mummy pads feel unstable when you're on your side; wider rectangular pads give you room to shift.
- **Weight and packed size.** If you're carrying the pad, a few ounces matter. Just know that the thickest, most comfortable pads are usually the heaviest, so match the pad to how far you're actually walking.

## FAQ

**How thick should a sleeping pad be for side sleepers?**
Aim for at least 3 inches, and ideally 4 inches if you're a heavier sleeper or sleep on hard ground. Thickness is what lets the pad absorb pressure at the hip and shoulder instead of passing it straight through to the ground.

**Do side sleepers need a higher R-value?**
Not necessarily higher than back sleepers, but compressed foam insulates poorly, so your hip area can feel colder. If you camp in cold weather, choose R-3.5 or higher, and consider a full-length insulated pad rather than a short one.

**Is a foam or air pad better for side sleeping?**
Foam and self-inflating pads are more stable and don't bounce, which some side sleepers prefer. Air pads are lighter and often thicker, but can feel bouncier. It comes down to whether you prioritize stability or pack weight.

**Will a wide pad help me stop rolling off at night?**
Yes — a wide rectangular pad gives you room to shift from side to side without hitting the edge. Mummy-shaped pads are lighter but narrower, so restless side sleepers often do better with a rectangular or wide version.

**Can I use a sleeping pad topper or pillow to help with side sleeping?**
A small inflatable pillow and a thin foam topper can help, but they won't fix a pad that's too thin. Start with a thick enough pad, then add a pillow for your shoulder and a small pad under your hip if you need extra relief.
