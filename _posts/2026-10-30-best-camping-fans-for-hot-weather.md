It's 11 p.m., the tent has been baking in the sun all day, and the air inside feels like it's been exhaled by a dog. You're lying on top of your sleeping bag instead of in it, the fly is zipped because of mosquitoes, and every time you drift off, a trickle of sweat wakes you back up. A small fan won't turn a July campsite into an air-conditioned hotel room, but it can move enough air across your skin to make sleep possible — and that's the whole game. I've spent enough sweltering nights under nylon to know the difference between a fan that actually helps and one that whirs politely while you sweat. Here's what's worth buying.

## What to look for

- **Airflow vs. noise.** A fan rated in the 2,000–3,000 CFM range is marketing nonsense for a tent fan; what matters is whether it moves enough air at a speed you can sleep through. Look for multiple speed settings and a low setting that's genuinely quiet.
- **Runtime and power source.** Check the stated battery life at low and high speed separately — manufacturers love to quote the low-speed number. USB-C rechargeable is now the standard; a fan that also runs on a power bank or AA batteries is a nice backup.
- **Mounting options.** A clip, a hook, a magnetic base, or a built-in stand all matter depending on whether you're hanging it from a tent loop, clipping it to a cot, or setting it on a table.
- **Size and weight.** You're carrying this. A 1–2 pound fan that folds flat is worth more than a powerful brick you resent on the hike in.

## FAQ

**How much airflow do I actually need in a tent?**
Enough to feel moving air on your skin at the low setting, which is what you'll sleep on. A small fan moving 100–200 CFM is plenty for a two-person tent; bigger numbers mostly matter for large tents, group areas, or outdoor use.

**Will a camping fan run all night on one charge?**
Most quality rechargeable fans will run 8–12 hours on low speed, which covers a night. Check the manufacturer's low-speed runtime spec rather than the high-speed one, and consider a fan that runs off a power bank or swappable battery if you're out for multiple nights.

**Can I use a regular home fan while camping?**
Yes, if you have shore power at an RV site or a big enough inverter. A home circulator like the Vornado moves more air and is quieter than most tent fans, but it needs AC power and isn't practical for hike-in trips.

**Are battery-powered fans safe to leave running in a tent overnight?**
Yes, as long as the fan is designed for camping use and you keep it away from loose fabric that could get pulled into the blades. Avoid running cheap fans unattended if the housing feels flimsy, and never charge a battery inside a sealed tent on a hot day.

**Do I need a fan with a built-in lantern?**
Only if you want to cut down on gear. A combo unit is convenient for minimalist campers, but dedicated fans generally move more air for the same weight, and dedicated lanterns usually give better light.
