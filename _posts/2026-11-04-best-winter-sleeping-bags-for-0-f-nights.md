## What to look for

- **Temperature rating honesty.** Look for the EN/ISO comfort rating, not just the "limit" or the marketing number on the stuff sack. A bag sold as "0°F" often has a comfort rating closer to 10–15°F, so match the comfort figure to how cold you actually sleep.
- **Fill type and fill power.** Down packs lighter and warmer per ounce, but it's useless when wet. Synthetic stays warm damp but is heavier and bulkier. For 0°F, look for 650–800+ fill-power down or a quality synthetic like Climashield.
- **Draft management.** A draft collar, insulated draft tube behind the zipper, and a well-shaped hood do more for real warmth than an extra ounce of fill. Cheap bags skimp here.
- **Weight vs. packability trade-off.** A true 0°F down bag runs roughly 3–4.5 lbs; synthetic versions push 5–7 lbs. Decide whether you're carrying it or sledding it before you buy.

## FAQ

**Is a 0°F sleeping bag overkill for most winter camping?**
For most people camping in the lower 48 in winter, a 0°F bag is more than enough and often more than you need — a 15°F or 20°F bag plus layers handles most trips. A true 0°F bag makes sense for high-elevation trips, the northern tier, or when you sleep cold and want a margin.

**Should I choose down or synthetic for a 0°F bag?**
Down wins on weight and packability and is the better choice if you can keep the bag dry. Synthetic stays warm when damp and is more forgiving in wet, humid cold, but it's heavier and bulkier — for a bag this warm, synthetic versions get very heavy fast.

**How do I know if a bag's 0°F rating is realistic?**
Look for the EN/ISO comfort rating rather than the "limit" or the marketing number. Boutique brands like Western Mountaineering and Feathered Friends tend to rate conservatively, while mass-market bags often quote optimistic numbers — read the comfort figure and match it to your sleep style.

**What pad should I pair with a 0°F bag?**
A winter bag only insulates the top; the bottom is compressed and relies on your pad. Use a pad with an R-value of at least 5, and consider stacking two pads or adding a closed-cell foam layer for real 0°F nights, since cold ground will undo a warm bag fast.

**Can I use a 0°F bag in warmer weather?**
Yes, but it's uncomfortable — you'll overheat and wake up sweaty, which then makes you cold. Some bags, like the NEMO Sonic 0, have venting features that help, but generally a 0°F bag is best reserved for genuinely cold trips rather than pushed into three-season use.
