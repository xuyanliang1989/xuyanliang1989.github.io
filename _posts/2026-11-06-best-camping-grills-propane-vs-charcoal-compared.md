It's the second night of a three-day trip, the coals you lit twenty minutes ago are still stubbornly black, and the hot dogs are sitting raw on a paper plate while everyone huddles around a dying campfire. Or maybe you went the other way — the propane grill fired up instantly, but the burgers came out tasting like a plain kitchen pan and you're wondering why you hauled it out here. The propane-versus-charcoal question is the first real fork in the road for anyone shopping for a camping grill, and it changes what you should actually buy. This guide compares both fuel types honestly, walks through the features that matter in the field, and rounds up real grills you can buy today at real prices.

## What to look for

- **Fuel logistics.** Propane means a 1-lb disposable cylinder or a refillable tank; charcoal means hauling briquettes and dealing with ash. Decide which hassle you'd rather carry.
- **Packed size and weight.** A camping grill lives in your trunk or pack. Look at folded dimensions and weight, not just cooking area.
- **Heat control.** Propane gives you a knob; charcoal gives you vent management and coal placement. Match the grill to how much fiddling you enjoy.
- **Cleanup and fire rules.** Some campgrounds and drought-stricken areas restrict open-flame charcoal. Check local rules before you buy, and factor in ash disposal.

## FAQ

**Is propane or charcoal better for camping?**
Propane is easier to light, control, and clean up, and many campgrounds allow it during fire restrictions. Charcoal delivers more smoke flavor and doesn't require a fuel cylinder, but it needs more time, ash disposal, and sometimes faces open-flame bans.

**Can I use a camping grill during a fire ban?**
It depends on the ban. Many jurisdictions allow contained propane grills with a shutoff valve even when open fires are prohibited, but charcoal is often restricted. Always check the specific campground or land agency rules before your trip.

**How much propane does a camping grill use?**
A standard 1-lb cylinder typically fuels a small portable grill for roughly an hour to an hour and a half of cooking, depending on the burner output and setting. For multi-day trips, bring a refillable tank and an adapter hose.

**Do I need to season a charcoal grill?**
Cast-iron grates and bodies benefit from a light coat of oil and a few heat cycles before first use to build a protective layer. Porcelain-enameled surfaces generally don't need seasoning but should be cleaned gently to avoid chipping.

**How do I dispose of charcoal ash at a campsite?**
Let the coals cool completely, then bag the ash and pack it out or use a designated ash bin — never dump hot ash into the woods or a trash can. Some campgrounds prohibit charcoal entirely, so confirm before you rely on it.
