It's 2 a.m., it's 38 degrees, and nature is calling. You unzip the tent fly, fumble for a headlamp, and start the muddy, half-asleep stumble toward a vault toilet that may or may not be a quarter mile away — or worse, toward a trowel and a cathole you'll have to dig in frozen ground. Anyone who has tent camped for more than a weekend knows this moment. A dedicated camping toilet doesn't just add convenience; it keeps late-night trips short, keeps your camp clean, and makes tent camping a lot more civilized, especially with kids, older parents, or a partner who is not thrilled about the bush. Below are the toilets that actually hold up in tents and at tent-adjacent campsites, plus what to look for before you buy.

## What to look for

- **Capacity and flush type.** A 5-gallon cassette or portable toilet handles roughly 2–3 people for a weekend before you need to empty it. A bucket-style toilet with bags is lighter but needs a fresh bag every use. Match capacity to your group size and how long you'll be out.
- **Height and stability.** A toilet that sits at roughly standard seat height (about 16–18 inches) is far easier on knees and backs than a low bucket, and a wide base matters on uneven tent-site ground. Look for a footprint that won't tip when someone shifts weight.
- **Odor and waste control.** Sealing lids, drop-in deodorizer packets, and double-seal valves make the difference between a tolerable camp and a smelly one. If you'll be packing waste out (required in many areas), confirm the toilet accepts standard wag bags or double-bagging.
- **Setup, weight, and packability.** A folding toilet that collapses flat stores in a car trunk or tote; a rigid portable toilet is bulkier but more comfortable. Check the packed weight if you're carrying it any distance from the vehicle.

## FAQ

**Can I really use a portable toilet inside a tent?**
Yes, but most people set it just outside the door or in the vestibule rather than fully inside the sleeping area — it keeps odors out of your sleeping space and gives you a bit more room. If you do use it inside, choose a model with a good sealing lid and keep a deodorizer in it.

**How often do I need to empty a portable camping toilet?**
A 5-gallon cassette or portable toilet typically handles two to three people for a weekend before it needs emptying, depending on use. Smaller bucket-style toilets with bags need a fresh bag every use, so plan your bag supply accordingly.

**What's the best way to control odor in a camping toilet?**
Use a sealing lid, drop in a deodorizer packet or a scoop of sawdust/kitty litter after each use, and empty the tank promptly rather than letting waste sit. Models with a water seal or gelling bags control odor far better than an open bucket.

**Do I have to pack out human waste when tent camping?**
It depends on where you are — many desert, alpine, and some national park backcountry areas require pack-out, while other areas allow catholes dug 6–8 inches deep and at least 200 feet from water. Check local regulations before your trip, and carry wag bags if pack-out is required.

**Are folding toilets stable enough for older campers?**
Folding toilets are generally stable once the legs lock, but they're less rigid than molded units on very uneven ground. For older campers or anyone with balance concerns, a rigid portable toilet like the Camco or Thetford is the safer, more comfortable choice.
