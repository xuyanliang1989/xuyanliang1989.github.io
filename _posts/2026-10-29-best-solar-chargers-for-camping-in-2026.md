## What to look for

**Real-world wattage, not marketing wattage.** A panel rated at 21W under perfect lab conditions might deliver a fraction of that under haze or at an angle. Look for panels with decent efficiency ratings and read owner reports on actual output, not just the sticker number.

**Battery or no battery.** A panel-only setup is lighter and cheaper but stops charging the moment a cloud rolls in. A built-in power bank lets you harvest sun all day and charge devices at night — heavier, but far more practical for multi-day trips.

**Ports and charging protocols.** USB-C PD output matters if you're charging a phone, tablet, or mirrorless camera quickly. Check how many ports it has and whether it can charge multiple devices at once without splitting power to a useless trickle.

**Packability and durability.** Folding panels with grommets and a decent IP rating survive being strapped to a pack or staked to the ground. Rigid glass panels are tougher but heavier; thin film is light but fragile.

## FAQ

**Can a solar charger actually charge a phone in 2026, or is it still too slow?**
Yes, but manage expectations. A 20–30W panel in direct sun can charge a modern phone at a reasonable rate, roughly comparable to a slow wall charger. Under clouds, haze, or at a bad angle, output drops sharply, so most experienced campers pair a panel with a battery bank and charge the bank all day.

**Do I need a solar charger with a built-in battery?**
It depends on your trip length. For a one-nighter, a panel with a small built-in battery like the BioLite SolarPanel 10+ is convenient because you can charge after dark. For multi-day trips, a separate larger battery bank paired with a panel-only charger usually gives you more total capacity for the weight.

**Will a folding solar panel survive rain?**
Most folding panels are weather-resistant but not fully waterproof, so a light drizzle is usually fine but a downpour is not. Check the IP rating before you buy, and plan to bring the panel inside or under cover during heavy rain. Rigid panels like the Renogy suitcase handle weather better but are much heavier.

**Can I charge a laptop with a camping solar charger?**
Only with a larger panel and the right output. A 100W panel like the Jackery SolarSaga or Renogy suitcase paired with a power station can charge a laptop via the station's AC outlet. Pocket panels and 30W folding units generally can't deliver enough wattage for a laptop, even with USB-C PD.

**How do I get the most out of a solar panel at camp?**
Angle the panel perpendicular to the sun and re-aim it every hour or two as the sun moves — a panel lying flat loses a surprising amount of output. Keep it out of partial shade, since even a small shadow across one cell can cut output dramatically. Panels with an ammeter or sundial, like the BigBlue and BioLite, make this much easier.
