It's 2 a.m. and the mosquitoes have found the one square inch of your shoulder pressed against the hammock fabric. You zip the net, but the zipper snags, and now you're wide awake, swatting at your own ear in the dark while the guy 30 feet away snores peacefully inside his fully sealed shelter. A hammock without a bug net isn't a shelter — it's a buffet with a view. The right netted hammock changes that: you hang between two trees, sleep in sealed comfort, and wake up without a constellation of bites on your ankles. This guide covers the netted hammocks that actually work, what separates a good one from a frustrating one, and which model fits how you camp.

## What to Look For

- **Integrated vs. separate net:** An attached, zippered net is faster to set up and harder to lose. Separate "Fronkey-style" nets are lighter and more versatile but add fiddle time at camp.
- **Weight and packed size:** Solo backpackers should aim under about 2 lbs for hammock plus net; car campers can carry a heavier, roomier rig without caring.
- **Net construction:** Look for fine no-see-um mesh (around 15-denier or finer) with a durable zipper and a ridge line that holds the net off your face.
- **Suspension included?:** Many hammocks ship bare. Tree straps and whoopie slings add cost and weight — factor them in before you compare prices.

## FAQ

**Do I need a separate tarp with a netted hammock?**
Most netted hammocks protect against bugs but not rain, so yes — unless the model includes a fly like the Hennessy or Kammok systems. Budget for a tarp if you plan to camp in wet weather.

**Can I use a netted hammock in cold weather?**
Yes, but the net alone won't keep you warm. You'll want an underquilt or pad beneath you, since compressed sleeping bags lose insulation value in a hammock.

**How do I keep the bug net off my face?**
Look for a hammock with an integrated or separate ridgeline that holds the mesh up. Models like the JungleNest and Skeeter Beeter Pro use this approach and are much more comfortable because of it.

**Are netted hammocks good for side sleepers?**
Some are. Asymmetric designs like the Blackbird XLC and Hennessy Expedition allow a diagonal lay that's far more comfortable for side sleepers than a basic gathered-end hammock.

**What's the lightest way to add bug protection?**
A standalone net like the Therm-a-Rest Slacker Bug Net paired with a lightweight hammock is usually the lightest and cheapest route, though it takes a bit more setup than an integrated net.
