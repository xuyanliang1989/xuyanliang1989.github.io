## What to look for

- **Temperature rating — and the comfort rating, not the limit.** Manufacturers list a "limit" rating (the temp a warm sleeper survives) and a "comfort" rating (the temp an average woman sleeps comfortably). For cold weather, buy to the comfort rating and add a margin of about 10°F.
- **Insulation type.** Down packs smaller and warmer per ounce but loses loft when wet and costs more. Synthetic holds warmth when damp and is cheaper, but it's bulkier and heavier. Under $100, most quality options are synthetic.
- **Fill weight and loft, not just the number on the tag.** A bag rated to 20°F with thin fill will sleep cold. Look at how much insulation is actually in the bag and whether it has a draft collar and hood.
- **Shape and pad attachment.** Mummy bags are warmer per ounce; rectangular bags are roomier but draftier. A bag that attaches to your pad (or at least has a full-length draft tube) stops cold air from sneaking in.

## FAQ

**What temperature rating should I buy for cold-weather camping under $100?**
Aim for a comfort rating around 20°F to 30°F if you're camping in fall or early spring, and a limit rating of 0°F to 15°F if you expect genuine winter conditions. Remember that budget bags often have optimistic ratings, so add a 10°F buffer to whatever the tag says.

**Is down or synthetic better for a budget cold-weather bag?**
Under $100, synthetic is almost always the better choice. Down bags at this price tend to have low fill power and thin shells, while synthetic insulation stays warm even if it gets damp and costs less. The tradeoff is weight and bulk, which matters more for backpacking than car camping.

**How do I stay warm in a cheap sleeping bag?**
Layer up with a warm base layer and a hat, use a sleeping pad with a decent R-value (at least 4 for cold weather), and add a liner or a fleece blanket inside the bag. A draft collar and hood make a bigger difference than most people expect, so cinch them down.

**Can I use a 30°F bag in freezing temperatures?**
Not comfortably. A 30°F bag is a three-season bag for mild nights, and pushing it into freezing temps means you'll sleep cold and possibly dangerously so. If you expect frost, choose a bag rated 15°F or lower and pair it with a warm pad.

**How often should I replace a budget sleeping bag?**
Synthetic bags typically lose loft and warmth after a few seasons of regular use, especially if they're stored compressed. Store it loosely in a cotton sack between trips, wash it gently once a season, and replace it when it stops lofting back up or you consistently sleep cold in conditions it used to handle.
