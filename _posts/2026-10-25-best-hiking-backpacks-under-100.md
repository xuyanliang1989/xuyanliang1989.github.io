## What to look for

- **Fit and torso length over features.** A $70 pack that fits your torso beats a $95 pack that doesn't. Measure your torso (C7 vertebra to the top of your hip bones) and check the pack's size chart before anything else.
- **Suspension and back panel.** Look for a framesheet or internal frame, a padded hipbelt that transfers weight to your hips, and some kind of ventilated or channeled back panel. Mesh back panels make a real difference in heat.
- **Weight vs. durability trade-off.** Ultralight budget packs often use thin fabrics that snag on brush. For general hiking, a 2–3 lb pack with 210D or heavier nylon in high-wear areas is a smarter buy than a 1 lb pack that tears.
- **Real capacity, not marketing capacity.** A "40L" pack that only holds 30L of usable space is common at this price. Check whether the listed liters include external pockets and whether the main body actually swallows a bear can or a 3-season sleep system.

## FAQ

**Is a 20L backpack enough for a day hike?**
For most day hikes, yes — 20 liters holds a 2–3 liter reservoir, lunch, a light layer, and a small first-aid kit. If you're carrying camera gear, a stove, or bulky layers, step up to 30–40 liters.

**Do I need a hipbelt on a daypack?**
For loads under about 15 lbs, a hipbelt is mostly about stability rather than weight transfer. Once you're carrying 20 lbs or more, a padded hipbelt becomes important for comfort, which is why the Trail 40 and Redwing 36 handle heavier loads better.

**How do I measure torso length for a backpack?**
Tilt your head forward and have someone measure from the bony bump at the base of your neck (C7 vertebra) straight down to the top of your hip bones. That number in inches is your torso length — match it to the pack's size chart rather than guessing by height.

**Can a sub-$100 backpack handle an overnight trip?**
Some can. The REI Trail 40 and Kelty Redwing 36 are both capable of a light overnight if you pack carefully and keep your base weight low. Packs in the 20-liter range are daypacks only.

**Are expensive backpacks worth it over budget ones?**
Above $100 you're mostly paying for lighter fabrics, more refined suspension, and better ventilation. For day hikes and light overnights, a well-fitting budget pack gets you 90% of the way there — fit matters far more than price.
