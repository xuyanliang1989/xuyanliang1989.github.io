## FAQ

**Are 4-person tents actually big enough for four people?**

Technically yes, but comfortably, no — most 4-person tents fit four sleeping bags wall-to-wall with no room for gear. If you're camping with four adults and duffels, size up to a 6-person tent, or plan to leave most gear in the car. For two adults and two kids, a 4-person tent is usually just right.

**Do I need a footprint or tarp under a budget tent?**

It's a good idea, especially with cheaper tents that have thin floors. A footprint protects against abrasion, pinholes, and ground moisture, and it's much cheaper than replacing a tent. You can buy a fitted footprint or just cut a tarp slightly smaller than the tent floor — make sure it doesn't stick out past the edges, or it'll channel water underneath.

**How do I keep a cheap tent from leaking in the rain?**

Seal the seams with a quality seam sealer before your first trip, and reapply as needed. Pitch the tent on high ground, guy it out so the fly doesn't touch the inner walls, and avoid touching the walls from inside during rain — contact points wick water through. A cheap tent with sealed seams and a good pitch handles a lot more weather than people expect.

**What's the difference between a 3-season and 4-season tent?**

Three-season tents are built for spring, summer, and fall — they prioritize ventilation and are fine for most camping. Four-season tents are heavier, stronger, and designed to shed snow and handle high winds, but they're often stuffier in summer. For camping under $200, almost everything you'll find is three-season, which is what most people actually need.

**Can I use a 4-person tent for backpacking?**

Most tents in this price range are too heavy and bulky for backpacking — they're car-camping tents. If you need a backpacking shelter, look for a tent under 5 pounds, which usually means spending more or dropping to a 2-person size. For short hikes to a campsite a mile or so in, a lighter 4-person tent can work, but check the packed weight before you commit.
