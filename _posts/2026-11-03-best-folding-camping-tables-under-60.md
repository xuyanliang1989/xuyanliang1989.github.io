## What to look for

- **Packed size and weight:** A table you dread carrying stays in the garage. Look for something that folds to roughly the size of a camp chair and weighs under 12 pounds if you're hiking it in or loading it into a small car.
- **Tabletop material:** Aluminum tops are light and wipe clean but can dent; high-density polyethylene (HDPE) tops handle heat and knife marks better but weigh more. Avoid thin MDF or particle board — it swells the first time it gets wet.
- **Frame and leg design:** Cross-braced aluminum legs with locking hinges are the sweet spot. Check the stated weight capacity and whether the legs have feet that won't sink into soft ground.
- **Height and adjustability:** Low tables (around 16 inches) are great for sitting on the ground or in low chairs; standard-height tables (around 28 inches) work with camp chairs. Some roll-top tables let you dial in height.

## FAQ

**Is a roll-top aluminum table strong enough for a camp stove?**
Yes, as long as the stove sits flat and the table is on level ground. Roll-top tables handle a two-burner camp stove fine, but avoid putting a heavy Dutch oven or a full cooler on the slats — the gaps and thin aluminum aren't built for concentrated weight.

**What's the difference between a low table and a standard-height camping table?**
Low tables (around 16 inches) work with ground chairs or sitting on a pad, and they're usually lighter. Standard-height tables (around 28 inches) pair with regular camp chairs and are more comfortable for full meals, but they're heavier and bulkier when packed.

**Can these tables handle rain overnight?**
The aluminum and HDPE tops on this list are weather-resistant and won't be ruined by rain, but roll-top tables let water through the slats. If you leave one out, flip it or tilt it so water doesn't pool, and dry the frame before storing it to keep the hinges from corroding.

**How much weight can a folding camping table hold?**
It varies widely — check the manufacturer's stated capacity. Most tables in this price range are rated for roughly 50 to 100 pounds, which covers plates, drinks, and a stove, but not a person sitting on it. The Lifetime table is the sturdiest of the group for heavier loads.

**Do I need a carry bag for a folding camping table?**
Most of these come with one, and it's worth using — the bag keeps the table from scratching other gear and makes it easier to carry along with chairs. If yours doesn't include a bag, a cheap duffel or stuff sack works fine.
